The whole purpose of the ketubah

A good wife is built on what she is given.

Women are encouraged to be good wives: to be kind and giving, to build their husband, to make a home. All of this is true.

And her ability to do it completely depends on receiving from her husband what she needs and deserves in a marriage. That is the entire purpose of the ketubah.

It is very hard to bring that to life until you understand what the ketubah is teaching you. With that understanding, you are empowered to build a beautiful marriage.

A young woman who knows what she deserves will never again confuse being wanted with being used.

Six classes. One whole picture.

01Knowing yourselfWhat You Deserve“What am I even looking for?”
  • Listen to yourself: what you most need to feel from him. The ketubah, read out loud, promise by promise.
  • “You are mine” means “you are my responsibility.” Why the Torah puts that first.
  • Safety does not start with a husband. It started in your father's house, and it is not weakness.
  • What this tells you about men: a man becomes himself by providing, protecting, giving and loving. Your needs are what he was made for.
02What you bringWhy You Deserve It“Why should he do all that for me?”
  • The myths of marriage, and the truth: it is meant to be the most fulfilling thing a person does.
  • What you bring: the incredible value you bring to your husband. Your words, your respect, your beauty, your loyalty.
  • A woman stepping into her full self: giving from fullness, not sacrifice.
  • Sod HaYichud: whatever you need, he needs, inverted. Two halves, built for each other.
03IntimacyThe Expression of Oneness, Safety, and Love“How do I know his desire is safe?”
  • What physical intimacy is in a marriage: the expression of the oneness everything else is building toward.
  • The two faces of a man's desire in this world, and what the wrong one does to a woman who meets it.
  • The healing: the only things that belong in intimacy are the things that make you safe. Anything else has no place.
  • What to put on the table before engagement, touch during dating, and why attraction is a condition of marriage.
04DynamicsWhat Makes or Breaks a Marriage“What actually destroys a marriage, and what repairs it?”
  • Male aggression and anger: friction you work out, versus fear, which is the answer.
  • Female character assassination: the same words that build a man can take him apart.
  • The silent treatment, and navigating differences of opinion and hashkafa.
  • When feelings were hurt: how to repair, on both sides.
05DatingDating and Building Friendship“What is dating actually for?”
  • Dating is not only discernment. It is how a relationship gets built, and how a man grows into it.
  • Having fun, and giving him opportunities to take care of you, and yourself opportunities to love him.
  • Having someone between you: when a middle person helps and when to go direct.
  • Long-term versus short-term dating, and what to check before you go out.
06DecidingChoosing the Right One“Is this a yes, or is it fear talking?”
  • Red flags in dating: when something could be a problem, what to look for and how to notice it.
  • The things that come up while dating that mean: look deeper before you decide.
  • Everything on the table: how to talk to him about a red flag.
  • Settling versus being picky, and how to break up.

Why I teach this

The Torah teaches it in detail.

For more than twenty years I have sat with couples, with chassanim, and with women carrying the pain of marriages that didn't work. Not bad people. Not bad marriages. Two people who were never shown how a husband and a wife actually fit together.

Most of what goes wrong was visible before the engagement. Not because anyone was hiding it, but because the young woman had never been taught what she was looking at, or that she was allowed to need anything at all.

The Torah teaches it in detail. It is in the ketubah, in the words of kiddushin, in the halachos every chassan learns and no kallah is told. I teach it to young women because it is theirs.

— Rav Yisrael Frenkel

Questions

Before you come

Do I have to talk in the circle?

No. Many just listen the first week. The circle is there for whoever wants it.

Is this a kallah class?

No. A kallah class comes later and teaches halacha. This course comes before, and teaches what you need to know before you say yes to anyone.

Is anything personal asked of me?

Nothing. You are never asked to share anything. Much of the course you will simply hear, and recognize.
